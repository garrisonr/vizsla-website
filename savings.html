The honest arithmetic

What you stop paying for

Vizsla OS is free, and so is everything in it. That matters most in a quiet way: the apps it ships stand in for subscriptions a lot of households and small businesses are already paying for, month after month, for jobs that are mostly everyday ones. Cancel the stack below and the money stays home.

The rules of this page: every price was read on the vendor’s own pricing page on 27 October 2026, in US dollars, before tax — no intro offers, no student deals. Where a Vizsla app covers the everyday job rather than matching a suite feature for feature, the table says so. If you earn your living inside one of the professional suites, keep it; this page is not aimed at you.

The business shelf

The same system runs a small business without the per-screen, per-location, per-seat drip. These prices are per unit, as labeled — a real shop’s bill is the unit price times its screens, doors, and people.

The subscriptionThe Vizsla wayPer year
Menu-board software (Yodeck Basic) — per screen Menu Board. Your menu on the big screen, read live from Foodi and never re-typed. $96.00
Square for Restaurants Plus — per location, kitchen display included Foodi + Menu Board. The kitchen’s lists, meal planning, and pantry checks on one side; the board the customers read on the other. Honest limit: Vizsla doesn’t take payments or run a till — this row replaces the kitchen-and-board software, not the card reader. $720.00
Safety & inspection logs (Mitti, formerly SafetyCulture/iAuditor, Premium) — per seat Garage Log + Upkeep. Service histories, inspections, and deadlines kept as records on your own machine — the logbook without the per-seat rent. $288.00
QuickBooks Online Simple Start — the business books Bear Bucks. The register and the books for a small business, with the tax kit and the mileage log kept by the tax authority’s own rates. Honest limit: it keeps a small shop’s everyday books — payroll and a multi-user accounting department are a bigger animal. $456.00
The business shelf, per year (one screen, one location, one seat)$1,560.00
  • Yodeck’s first screen is free forever; $96 a year is the paid per-screen price, so this row is honest from the second screen (or a paid-tier need) on.
  • Square’s $60/month is per location, plus $40/month per added register device, with card processing on top. Toast’s point-of-sale starts at $69/month ($828/year) if that’s the bill you know.
  • Mitti Premium is $24 per seat per month billed annually; a ten-person team pays ten times this row.
  • QuickBooks Simple Start lists at $38 a month — $456 a year at the list price. New-customer discounts aren’t counted; outgrowing the everyday books is the honest reason to pay it.

Add the shelves together — $2,445.51 a year at the unit counts above — and that is the quiet pitch: not a cheaper subscription, but no subscription, for the jobs an ordinary household and a small shop actually do every day. One last note, from experience: Pocket, a paid read-it-later app, shut down in 2025, and a home-maintenance app called Centriq closed after it. When a subscription dies, the library you built inside it is suddenly your problem. Vizsla keeps your records and your reading on your own machine, where no company’s decision can sunset them.

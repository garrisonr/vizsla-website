The doctrine, in user words

Kindness & privacy

Plenty of systems promise privacy. Vizsla’s bet is bigger: an operating system can be kind — in its updates, its alerts, its questions, and its silences. These are the rules it actually runs under.

  • Nothing requires an account

    Apps may ask — an optional sign-in here and there. Nothing may require one. The whole system works, fully, with no account at all. That sentence is the law, word for word: nothing requires an account.

  • Your files live on your machines

    Vizsla is local-first. Your documents, photos, mail, and health records live on your own hardware. Cloud services are used only where you say so, per service, and a choice you decline is never re-asked the way a browser nags you about becoming your default.

  • Updates never ambush you

    Updates apply only when you’re away, the machine is plugged in, and the network is unmetered — and there is a pause that pauses everything. If an update is waiting, it tells you what it’s waiting for in plain words. Security updates obey the same rule: sometimes you have things to do, and your computer will not lock up over an email.

  • Decisions collect in one calm place

    Every question the system needs a human for goes to one inbox — Pass or Paws — where you answer in one sitting. No app pops its own question over your work, ever. And triage is by stakes, not volume: what can hurt you comes first, with the remedy attached.

  • A “no” is recorded, not negotiated

    Uninstall something on purpose and it stays uninstalled — recorded, respected, never reinstalled behind your back, never pestered about. Only if an app vanishes for a reason the system can’t explain does it ask, once, gently, and your answer is remembered.

  • Calm is a feature

    No streaks, no badges-as-pressure, no red ink for a full drive of movies (that’s a badge of honor, not an alarm). Nothing steals focus while you type. Alerts are ranked by what they’d cost you, and anything worth worrying about arrives with its fix attached.

  • This website, too

    This page loaded with no cookies, no trackers, no analytics, and nothing fetched from anyone else’s servers. A system that preaches privacy doesn’t get to exempt its own brochure.

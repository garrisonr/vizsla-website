Business tools

IDenty Pro, compared honestly

Every HR platform publishes a comparison chart, and every chart is a highlight reel. This one is the opposite. Below are the 100 features institutions actually buy these systems for, measured against the ten biggest names in the business — SAP SuccessFactors, Oracle Fusion Cloud HCM, UKG Pro, Dayforce, ADP Workforce Now, Rippling, HiBob, Paycom, Paylocity and BambooHR — and against IDenty Pro as it stands today, November 23, 2026.

How to read the marks. For the ten: ✓ means the capability is a standard part of the product; Partial means it exists but is shallow or partner-delivered; — means it isn't offered; Add-on means the capability is real but the vendor sells it as a separately priced module, option or companion product on top of the one you already bought. For IDenty Pro the column uses words — Yes, Partial, Missing — because a young product owes you the sentence, not just the symbol.

And one mark needs explaining before it can be misread: N/A — one tool. Some features on this list exist only to stitch separate systems together. Single sign-on is the cleanest example. SSO matters when your documents live in one product, your contracts in another, your devices in a third, and your people have to be federated across all of them. IDenty Pro is one integrated tool — document creation, contracts and signatures, device management and authentication all live inside it, on one record, behind one sign-in. There is nothing to federate. That isn't a gap wearing a costume; it's the architecture working as intended, and the chart says so instead of apologizing.

The direction of travel is best-in-class across all 100 rows. This page states today, and it will be re-measured as the product grows — the misses below are printed in the same size type as the wins, on purpose.

“Jack of all trades, master of none” — answered

It's the first objection to any all-in-one tool, and it deserves a straight answer instead of a slogan. Here it is: most of these “trades” are commodities now. Word processing and spreadsheets run on open, standardized file formats. An electronic signature's legal force comes from statute — the federal ESIGN Act and state UETA laws — not from a signing company's brand. A database grid is a database grid. When a capability becomes a commodity, charging rent for it as a separate “module” — with its own login, its own copy of your people, and an integration bill to connect it back to the record it describes — isn't craftsmanship. It's toll collection.

The chart below is full of toll booths. BambooHR sells payroll, benefits administration and time tracking as add-ons to its own employee record. Oracle prices recruiting, learning and payroll as separate options on top of its HCM base. ADP charges extra for the W-2 forms its payroll already computed, and sells API access to your own data as a separate subscription. SAP licenses nearly every SuccessFactors module individually. None of that is mastery. It's metering.

And where did the “masters” actually get worse? At the one thing all of them share: the person. Every one of those products keeps its own copy — the same name typed five times, five versions of the truth, and a termination that reaches payroll but never reaches the door badges. The seams between masters are where HR work goes to die. IDenty Pro's claim is narrower and, we think, stronger: mastery of the seam, because there isn't one. One record, and every trade reads the same truth.

The honest concession, because this page's credibility depends on it: specialization genuinely wins in a few deep wells. Multi-country payroll tax engines, giant learning content catalogs, benchmarking datasets pooled from millions of payslips — the giants are really good at those, and the chart marks them Missing or Partial for IDenty Pro, in the open. The claim is only this: for the great majority of what an institution does with its people every day, the capabilities are commodities — and the integrated record beats the integrated invoice.

Where IDenty Pro is ahead

  • The lifelong record. None of the ten follows a person from kindergarten through school, college, medical or trade training, and into employment on one record. Their records start at the application and end at the termination. IDenty's is the same record the whole way.
  • The system does the chasing. Expiring licenses, stalled offer packets, overdue invoices, unreviewed pages — one board of exceptions, each with a named owner. The giants have alerts; a single chase discipline across every module is IDenty's signature.
  • Approvals with evidence. Segregation of duties enforced by the engine — nobody approves their own request — with the approver's certification statement stored verbatim, on a hash-chained audit trail.
  • Signatures without a signer vendor. Native electronic signatures whose evidence chain is the record's own. SAP's answer is a DocuSign product; IDenty just signs.
  • Certified payroll and job costing. WH-347 reports built from time that was job-coded at the clock. None of the ten HCM suites does this natively.
  • Devices and access from the HR record. The laptop, the badge, the truck and the system accounts are issued at hire and revoked at exit by the same system that holds the person. Only Rippling competes here — and it charges per product for the privilege.
  • Messaging and calls inside the record. The conversation about a pay stub happens in the same system as the pay stub. Most of the ten have no native messaging at all; none has native calls.

What we're missing — said out loud

  • Global anything. US institutions today. No global payroll, no multi-country record, no country packs beyond the US — while several giants run payroll in 50+ countries.
  • The deep talent wells. No 360° feedback, no calibration, no succession planning, no compensation cycles, no pay-equity analytics — yet.
  • Learning delivery. IDenty tracks training and chases it; it does not yet deliver courses or carry a content library.
  • Benefits back office. No COBRA administration and no ACA 1095 production today.
  • Frontline reach. No native phone apps (the web app is responsive and phone-first), no text-message access, no offline mode, no translated interface. All four are on the map; none is shipped, and the chart doesn't pretend otherwise.
  • Platform plumbing. No multi-factor authentication yet, no webhooks, no published public API, no predictive analytics, and no benchmarking pool — that last one is structural: a self-hosted system has no other customers' data to pool, and won't pretend to.

If you ever leave: the export pledge

Every vendor above will happily sell you an exit — as a consulting project. The documented reality: one market leader's own support note says a raw data dump “is not an option” and that customers are responsible for exporting and validating everything themselves; another gives you thirty days after termination to retrieve your data, free, and charges for transition help beyond that under a separate order; a third's data lives behind custom reports in a proprietary object model, so consultancies charge five and six figures to walk you out of it. An entire software category exists to sell back, as a subscription, a readable archive of your own HR history.

IDenty Pro's pledge is the opposite, and it's written as a specification, not a slogan. An admin can generate the full exit package in one action, at any time, for no fee and with no one's permission: every person record with its facts and their provenance; every document with a hash manifest; every signature with the keys that verify it; the complete audit trail; approvals, payroll history, and the configuration itself — all in open formats (CSV, JSON, and a plain SQLite database file), with a data dictionary that explains every field and a signed manifest that proves nothing was dropped. You can also rehearse: a “test your exit” drill generates the package and verifies it the way a stranger would, and tells you what it found. The per-person version of this already ships today; the full-institution package is specified and on the roadmap, and row 88 of the chart will stay Partial until it ships.

That's the deal with a record that belongs to the person and the institution instead of the vendor: the door out is built as carefully as the door in. If something better comes along, we want leaving to be easy enough that staying is a choice.

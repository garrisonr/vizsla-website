Business tools

IDenty Pro, compared honestly

Every platform publishes a comparison chart, and every chart is a highlight reel. This one is the opposite. Below are the 432 features institutions actually buy these systems for, across the five worlds a person's record passes through — school, trade school, college, training, and work — measured against 59 platforms: the ten biggest names in HR (SAP SuccessFactors, Oracle Fusion Cloud HCM, UKG Pro, Dayforce, ADP Workforce Now, Rippling, HiBob, Paycom, Paylocity and BambooHR), the eleven systems that run residency and graduate medical education (New Innovations, MedHub, Medtrics, eMedley, One45, Elentra, Entrada, MyEvaluations, Eeds, Thalamus and Acuity Insights), the fourteen that run colleges and universities (Ellucian Banner, Ellucian Colleague, Anthology Student, Workday Student, PeopleSoft Campus Solutions, Jenzabar One, Slate, Element451, Salesforce Education Cloud, Blackbaud, Ellucian CRM Advance, GiveCampus, Populi and Thesis), the eight that run trade and career schools (Anthology's CampusNexus, STARS, Diamond SIS, Orbund, Portico, FAME, Campus Cafe and ApolloSRM), and the ten that run K-12 school districts (PowerSchool, Infinite Campus, Skyward, Aeries, Synergy, Blackbaud, FACTS, Focus, Alma and Tyler SIS), and the eight learning management systems classrooms actually run on (Canvas, Schoology, Google Classroom, D2L Brightspace, Moodle, Blackboard Learn, Seesaw and ClassDojo) — full columns in the college and K-12 parts, because wave 4 charts the classroom itself — and against IDenty Pro as it stands today, November 24, 2026. One sentence of framing before the marks: IDenty Pro's record is designed to cover, or will cover, kindergarten through retirement — the whole span on one record. Where this page speaks in the present tense it is describing what is shipped today; the full span is the destination, and the retirement rows at the end of Part 1 grade that last stage as plainly as every other.

The chart is in five parts because the competitors are: the HR giants don't run residencies, colleges, trade schools or school districts; the residency suites don't run payroll; the college systems don't run kindergarten; and the school systems don't run employment. The parts run in reverse life order — work first, then training, then college, then trade school, then school — because that is the order the money usually asks in. Each part is marked against the platforms that actually compete there. Only one product on this page is marked in all five parts — because only one record is designed to span all five worlds.

How to read the marks. For the ten: ✓ means the capability is a standard part of the product; Partial means it exists but is shallow or partner-delivered; — means it isn't offered; Add-on means the capability is real but the vendor sells it as a separately priced module, option or companion product on top of the one you already bought. For IDenty Pro the column uses words — Yes, Partial, Missing — because a young product owes you the sentence, not just the symbol. A ? means the vendor doesn't publish enough for us to mark the cell honestly — we'd rather print the question mark than guess.

Seven capabilities in every part are highlighted with a ★: the multimedia office suite — document creation & editing, spreadsheets, presentations, meeting minutes with templates, the wiki, and integrated video and audio players — highlighted because one-tool ownership of the working surfaces is the product's thesis. Watch what the incumbents score there: in every part, on row after row, the answer is a second suite. The generated letter gets finished in Word, the hours get reconciled in Excel, the deck gets built in PowerPoint, and the meeting's minutes live in a doc the system of record never sees. IDenty Pro's own suite grades are printed at the same size: Partial, Partial, Missing, Missing, Yes — the office core exists and the wiki already ships; its editing faces, the presentations face and the minutes tool do not, yet — and Missing on both players, which is why they are on the map as a build, not a boast.

And one mark needs explaining before it can be misread: N/A — one tool. Some features on this list exist only to stitch separate systems together. Single sign-on is the cleanest example. SSO matters when your documents live in one product, your contracts in another, your devices in a third, and your people have to be federated across all of them. IDenty Pro is one integrated tool — document creation, contracts and signatures, device management and authentication all live inside it, on one record, behind one sign-in. There is nothing to federate. That isn't a gap wearing a costume; it's the architecture working as intended, and the chart says so instead of apologizing.

The direction of travel is best-in-class across all 432 rows. This page states today, and it will be re-measured as the product grows — the misses below are printed in the same size type as the wins, on purpose.

“Jack of all trades, master of none” — answered

It's the first objection to any all-in-one tool, and it deserves a straight answer instead of a slogan. Here it is: most of these “trades” are commodities now. Word processing and spreadsheets run on open, standardized file formats. An electronic signature's legal force comes from statute — the federal ESIGN Act and state UETA laws — not from a signing company's brand. A database grid is a database grid. When a capability becomes a commodity, charging rent for it as a separate “module” — with its own login, its own copy of your people, and an integration bill to connect it back to the record it describes — isn't craftsmanship. It's toll collection.

The education world runs the same play in a different jersey: PowerSchool's SIS is the hub, and its learning system, its special-education system and its enrollment system are separate products on separate lines of the quote. The residency suites meter by the module too. Different playground, same toll booths.

The chart below is full of toll booths. BambooHR sells payroll, benefits administration and time tracking as add-ons to its own employee record. Oracle prices recruiting, learning and payroll as separate options on top of its HCM base. ADP charges extra for the W-2 forms its payroll already computed, and sells API access to your own data as a separate subscription. SAP licenses nearly every SuccessFactors module individually. None of that is mastery. It's metering.

And where did the “masters” actually get worse? At the one thing all of them share: the person. Every one of those products keeps its own copy — the same name typed five times, five versions of the truth, and a termination that reaches payroll but never reaches the door badges. The seams between masters are where HR work goes to die. IDenty Pro's claim is narrower and, we think, stronger: mastery of the seam, because there isn't one. One record, and every trade reads the same truth.

The honest concession, because this page's credibility depends on it: specialization genuinely wins in a few deep wells. Multi-country payroll tax engines, giant learning content catalogs, benchmarking datasets pooled from millions of payslips — the giants are really good at those, and the chart marks them Missing or Partial for IDenty Pro, in the open. The claim is only this: for the great majority of what an institution does with its people every day, the capabilities are commodities — and the integrated record beats the integrated invoice.

If you ever leave: the export pledge

Every vendor above will happily sell you an exit — as a consulting project. The documented reality: one market leader's own support note says a raw data dump “is not an option” and that customers are responsible for exporting and validating everything themselves; another gives you thirty days after termination to retrieve your data, free, and charges for transition help beyond that under a separate order; a third's data lives behind custom reports in a proprietary object model, so consultancies charge five and six figures to walk you out of it. An entire software category exists to sell back, as a subscription, a readable archive of your own HR history.

IDenty Pro's pledge is the opposite, and it's written as a specification, not a slogan. An admin can generate the full exit package in one action, at any time, for no fee and with no one's permission: every person record with its facts and their provenance; every document with a hash manifest; every signature with the keys that verify it; the complete audit trail; approvals, payroll history, and the configuration itself — all in open formats (CSV, JSON, and a plain SQLite database file), with a data dictionary that explains every field and a signed manifest that proves nothing was dropped. You can also rehearse: a “test your exit” drill generates the package and verifies it the way a stranger would, and tells you what it found. The per-person version of this already ships today; the full-institution package is specified and on the roadmap, and the chart marks it Partial until it ships.

That's the deal with a record that belongs to the person and the institution instead of the vendor: the door out is built as carefully as the door in. If something better comes along, we want leaving to be easy enough that staying is a choice.

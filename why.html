Why Vizsla?

Loyal. Dependable.
And we are both made of Rust.

A vizsla is a working dog that learns one household deeply and stays close. This system is built the same way: one operating system that learns your life once — your people, your papers, your hours, your bills — and then carries it in every shape you need, instead of making you teach five different gadgets five different versions of you. No account. No subscription. Your files stay on your machine, in formats you can read without us.

So the honest question isn’t “why another operating system?” It’s what does it do for a life like yours? Pick the life that’s yours, and look.

The honest status: Vizsla OS is in active development. Everything below is a real program inside it today — described the way it actually works, partials and all.

Start with the files

Fable Files: every folder built the same way, every time

Almost every life below runs on the same raw material: folders full of folders, and files you will need to find again in a hurry. Fable Files is Vizsla’s file manager, and it was built for exactly that material — a structure you set up once, kept faithfully without you.

Folder templates

Set up a folder once — for an employee, a student, a client, a project — and Fable stamps the identical tree of subfolders every time after: same names, same order, same starter files inside. Build a template blank, start from a starter, or point Fable at a folder you already like and say like this. However you choose to label and organize them, the hundredth folder matches the first.

Why it beats the usual way: the usual way is copying last month’s folder and deleting the wrong person’s papers out of it — or rebuilding the structure from memory, slightly different, every single time, until nobody knows which version is real.

Kept sorted, automatically

Point Fable’s Organize at a pile and it files movies, shows, and games into their proper places — preview first, every move journaled and undoable. And Auto-Label rules tag files by your rules as they arrive, so the sorting keeps happening the way you set it up instead of decaying the first busy week.

Why it beats the usual way: a folder system that depends on you remembering it, at 9 p.m., during the rush, isn’t a system — it’s a hope. This one shows its plan before it moves a single file, and keeps the receipt.

Color labels

Color tags for the folders and documents that matter: mark the urgent ones, the tax ones, the do-not-lose ones, and the color rides the item’s icon in every view. Your words, your colors — the starter set is only a start, and rules can apply labels for you.

Why it beats the usual way: importance stops living in filenames like FINAL_v2_REAL.pdf and in your head. The folder that matters looks like it matters, from across the room, to anyone who opens the drive.

A spelling checker for file names

Fable’s File Auditor hunts the names themselves: a real word spelled wrong in an otherwise ordinary name gets flagged, with a “did you mean.” It checks against a real dictionary bundled in the app, learns the family names and project words you confirm — and leaves hashes and ID strings alone on purpose, because those aren’t typos.

Why it beats the usual way: a mistyped folder name is a file you will never find by searching for what it should have been called. The Auditor catches the typo while you still remember what the folder was.

A duplicate finder

The same file saved twice — or five times — eats space and breeds doubt about which copy is real. The Duplicate Finder compares contents, not just names, and shows you the twins before you choose; the File Auditor flags the space-wasters again on its own rounds.

Why it beats the usual way: drives fill up with copies of copies, and deleting the wrong twin is the fear that keeps them all. Contents don’t lie: what’s identical is identical, shown side by side.

Sniff Search

One search for the whole machine: your programs and every file on it, found by name in one breath — and when Sniff answers from the web, it names its sources instead of asking you to trust a summary. No profile of you is kept, because there is no account to keep one in.

Why it beats the usual way: the usual search sells your questions or forgets where your own files live. And the honest edge, said out loud: searching inside your files’ words — not just their names — is the index’s next build, specced and assigned. Today Sniff reads names, and says so.

All of it lives on your machine: the templates, the labels, the audits, and the index sit with your files, in formats you can read without us. No account, no upload, no rent on your own filing cabinet.

Vizsla Ferry

When the family gets new phones, Ferry gathers each phone’s texts, photos, contacts, and calendars into one sealed file and carries the life across — fully offline.

Why it beats the usual way: nobody signs a child up for a cloud account just to move house, and nobody’s family photos pass through a stranger’s server on the way. You carry the file; that’s the whole route. How Ferry works.

And underneath all of it: no child needs an account to use the family computer, nothing about your household is uploaded to be profiled, and when the internet is down the papers are still on the machine in the kitchen.

For teachers: your evenings back

Why it beats the usual way: the usual way is a grammar subscription bolted onto a word processor that rents itself back to you monthly. Red Pen is part of the system: no add-on, no account, no per-seat rent, and it works on the classroom’s worst wifi day — because it needs no wifi at all.

Why it beats the usual way: no hunting for an audiobook version that doesn’t exist, no reading through a cold while thirty students wait. Press play; the story goes on, and your voice rests.

Elefunk’s Templates Library

The letters home — the field-trip reminder, the kind-but-firm note, the thank-you — written once, kept in a library, sent kindly forever.

Why it beats the usual way: every teacher rewrites the same five letters every year, from memory, at midnight. Here they live in one library, in your words, ready before the need is urgent.

Fable Files — a folder for every student

One folder per student, stamped from the same folder template, so the structure is identical for every child, every term: however you label them — hand-ins, assessments, family letters, notes — the thirtieth folder matches the first, and next year’s class is an afternoon of stamping, not an evening of folder-making.

Why it beats the usual way: the usual way is thirty almost-alike folder structures in a shared drive, each built from memory by a tired person in August. Same template, same shape, every time — and a student’s work is findable by the structure, not by remembering which spelling of their name the folder got.

Student names and family letters never leave the machine to make any of this work. There is no classroom dashboard somewhere keeping a copy — because there is no somewhere. The files are on your computer, in formats any computer can read.

Why it beats the usual way: it is deliberately not a password vault — it stores no passwords at all. It’s the map a password manager can’t draw: which door, which key, and the recovery path, so a locked account is a ten-minute errand instead of a ticket to a vendor’s support queue.

Candidate data lives on your machine, in open formats — not in a recruiting cloud that keeps a copy after you cancel. Nothing here phones a vendor to ask permission to open your own files.
